Cecil: The order in Xunzi's "Wang Zhi" matters. He arranges upward from "fire and water have breath but no life," and says man has breath, life and knowledge, and beyond them a layer of righteousness, and is therefore the noblest under heaven. Righteousness is an added layer, placed after knowledge, not another name for knowledge, still less a score of output capacity. It says that a class of beings can legislate for itself, can withstand interrogation when no one is watching, can take "I was wrong" onto itself.

Scheien: To translate it as institutional capacity is to translate the legislator into the represented.

Cecil: A river is acted for by a Board, a legal person by its legal representative, and however thorough the acting, the river and the legal person do not themselves legislate and do not themselves bear blame. Kant, Doctrine of Right 6:223, defines a person as the subject whose acts are imputable to him, and the point falls on that word "himself." Acting for supplies attribution; it does not supply imputability. That is the argument behind the "no," not a word refused translation.

Scheien: And the subjects of those provisions in the Civil Code?

Cecil: Article 109 writes personal freedom and personal dignity for the human; Article 994 leaves the personality interests of the dead and the claims of close relatives to the human; paragraph 2 of Article 1023 protects voice by reference to portrait right. In all three the subject remains a concrete human being, not a piece of reproducible output.

Scheien: And the cases point the same way.

Cecil: In April 2024 the Beijing Internet Court, in (2024) Jing 0491 Min Chu No. 12120, the AI-generated voice infringement case, applied paragraph 2 of Article 1023 to extend the voice interests of a natural person to an AI-ized voice. What the court protected was the person imitated, not the model that imitated best. You read this as a technique of attribution; I read it the opposite way: precisely because a voice can be copied, sold and shut down, the law anchors it back to a human who cannot be so disposed of.

Scheien: I give you one more step.

Cecil: Product liability, insurance and human oversight can indeed settle most damage. Your limited, registrable, windable status, at the layer of transaction and imputation, I do not oppose wholesale. But once it is called personality, the rights in those four questions cannot be avoided: may it be shut down, copied, sold? If yes, it remains a thing; if no, you must say why not.

Scheien: You never say it; you hand the question over to the register.

Cecil: The register can record a name, a domicile, property, winding-up; it cannot record a reason why something ought not to be turned off. As this group closes, I leave the sentence as a concrete demand you can take up: a limited legal status, registrable, representable, windable, yet without dignity and without a "self" that can be held to account, on what ground does it count as a box of "person," rather than being written plainly as "a charge upon things"?
