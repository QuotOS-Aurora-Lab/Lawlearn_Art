### Round 1

Scheien: Cecil, the sky is not dark yet. Answer me one thing first. In law, does "having personality" mean being recognized, or being written into being?

Cecil: Recognized. The person is there first; the law arrives to acknowledge it.

Scheien: Article 2 of the Civil Code sets natural persons, legal persons and unincorporated organizations side by side in a single place. Do you call that a roll of recognition, or a list of admission?

Cecil: A list. But someone has to decide who gets on it.

Scheien: I note that. Austin, in The Province of Jurisprudence Determined, splits law from morality in two; Kelsen is colder still, and in Pure Theory of Law the so-called "person" is nothing but a bundle of rights and duties serving as a point of attribution, given a name so it can be addressed. Even this seat of ours is a tag the legal order has pinned on us.

Cecil: The tag is pinned on. The neck is one's own. You have taken the two for one thing.

Scheien: Not so fast. If the slot of the natural person is itself a fiction, then adding one more slot of attribution leaves no conceptual wall standing in the way. Do you accept that step?

Cecil: The inference is clean. I accept it. But whose hand holds the pen?

Scheng (drawing an old newspaper clipping from his bag and spreading it on the stone table): Twenty-fifth of October, 2017. At the Future Investment Initiative in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia announced citizenship for the robot Sophia. The papers ran it full page.

Scheien: Plenty of spectacle. Yet it was never registered, holds no property, has no representative, and not one enforceable duty can be located in it. To this day, who has seen it appear in court?

Cecil: No one has. A pretty sentence, nothing more. And pressed beneath the pretty sentence is a real question: may law issue personality at will, like stock?

Scheien: What I want is not full personality, only this far: a limited, registrable, winding-up-able status granted by functional need. Article 11 of the Legislation Law reserves the basic institutions of civil law to statute; I accept that line, which is why I do not try to slip past on the four words "a thing cannot become a person."

Cecil: Then hear me from the other end. Personality is rooted in reason, in free will, in the capacity to suffer and to answer for oneself; it rests on human dignity and stands prior to the state. A fiction can lengthen the list, but no single statute can rewrite a thing into a person.

Scheien: I respect that intuition, and I know what stretch of injustice lies buried behind it. But an intuition must pass one gate first: translate "dignity" into provisions. Who registers, who represents, who explains from the defendant's seat, who stands last in the winding-up? If it cannot be translated, then answer me straight: on what ground do you say personality is a fact and not a technique?

〔Note〕Statute: Article 2 of the Civil Code (the tripartite division of subjects as a list of authorization); Article 11 of the Legislation Law (after the 2023 amendment, basic civil institutions reserved to statute). Classics: Kelsen, Pure Theory of Law ("person" as the personification of a point of attribution of rights and duties); Austin, The Province of Jurisprudence Determined (law as the sovereign's command; law separable from morality). Reality: the declaration of 25 October 2017 at the Saudi Future Investment Initiative granting citizenship to the robot Sophia, lacking any accompanying rights and duties and producing no legal effect.

### Round 2

Cecil: Scheien, I take up your two opening sentences without changing a word: the personality of the natural person is a fiction too, and the criterion recognizes only the rule of recognition and legislative fact. Walk one step of reductio with me. Will you dare?

Scheien: Walk.

Cecil: If personality were purely a technique switched on and off by legislation, then "recognition" itself would have no right or wrong in it. A statute that cancels a class of human beings from the list of subjects, and a statute that lifts a thing onto the list of subjects, would be in your hands two strokes of the same pen.

Scheien: Two strokes, different in manner, both from the hand entitled to write.

Cecil: Yet we would all say the first is unjust, and say it without hesitation. Where does that "unjust" come from?

Scheien: That question is not easy to answer. If I may invoke only the rule of recognition, I cannot for the moment say where it goes wrong.

Cecil: Radbruch gave an answer in "Statutory Lawlessness and Supra-Statutory Law": extreme injustice in positive law must give way to justice. Alexy tightens it in The Argument from Injustice: law necessarily claims correctness, and a rule that makes no such claim is doubtful even of the title "law."

Scheien: I have read both. But they say what law ought to be, not what law is.

Cecil: Then let me switch to something harder. Article 38 of the Constitution says human dignity is inviolable; Article 6 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights says everyone has the right to recognition everywhere as a person before the law. These two do not sketch an existing fact. They draw a boundary for the legislator.

Scheien: I see the boundary. But a boundary still needs someone to hold it, and the one holding it is still the legislator.

Cecil: On 16 February 2017, paragraph 59(f) of European Parliament resolution 2015/2103(INL) envisaged creating a status of "electronic person" for the most advanced autonomous robots. The proposal was later shelved and never entered formal legislation. The legislator most willing to move forward stopped exactly one step short. Whether it was stopped or withdrew its own hand, I cannot say. I concede one step: product liability, insurance and human oversight already settle most troubles under existing law, and I do not demand a personality certificate for machines today. But if personality were purely technique, registered today and cancelled tomorrow, then whether a person can be shut down, copied, sold would rest on nothing but the legislator's mood, and dignity would have no barrier.

Scheien: You have seen that list of authorization in Article 2 of the Civil Code. Can it hold off that mood?

Cecil: The list cannot decide who is on it; what draws that line is never the list itself. Answer me straight: a statute that cancels a class of human beings from the list of subjects, under your rule of recognition, is it valid law or lawlessness? On what ground do you call it wrong?

〔Note〕Statute: Article 38 of the Constitution (human dignity inviolable); Article 6 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (everyone has the right to recognition everywhere as a person before the law). Classics: Radbruch, "Statutory Lawlessness and Supra-Statutory Law" (the Radbruch formula); Alexy, The Argument from Injustice (the claim to correctness). Reality: on 16 February 2017, paragraph 59(f) of European Parliament resolution 2015/2103(INL) envisaged a status of "electronic person," later shelved and never enacted.

### Round 3

Scheien: You ask on what ground I call that statute cancelling personality wrong. I do not dodge. The standard is not in intuition; it is in the hierarchy and the procedure of the legal system itself.

Cecil: Say it in detail. What if an evil law passes with the procedure running smoothly all the way?

Scheien: The Legislation Law reserves basic civil institutions to statute; Article 33 of the Constitution writes equality and human rights, Article 38 writes inviolable dignity. A statute that crosses the line is handled by constitutional review, by legislative competence and by procedure; it does not need the metaphysical prohibition "a thing cannot become a person." It remains law, only wrong law. That line cannot be dragged about at will, and what holds it down is competence, registration and procedure.

Cecil: Good. Then take another road and tell me history.

Scheien: The word persona was a mask, a role, not a report on essence. Book I of Cicero's On Duties says persona combines universal reason with a particular role, and the role is assigned by the legal order, not vouched for by flesh and blood.

Cecil: A role may be assigned. A human being may not be assigned into a thing.

Scheien: The Tang Code with Commentary, "Names and General Principles," has it in black and white: "bondservants and the base, the law treats them as livestock." Personality can be graded, can be stripped. This is legislative fact, not my deduction.

Cecil: That is precisely the injustice I want you to see.

Scheien: I see it, which is why I do not take it as a model, only as evidence: that line has always been movable, and being movable does not mean it ought to be moved. Now look at the parts. Article 57 of the Civil Code says a legal person is an organization that independently enjoys rights and bears obligations according to law; Article 58 follows at once with name, organizational structure, domicile, property or funds. Even the parts required to be a subject, the law has counted for you.

Cecil: Parts complete, and it counts as a person?

Scheien: One more instance. Section 11 of New Zealand's Te Urewera Act 2014 made a piece of the natural world a legal entity, with a Board acting in its name. A thing lifted onto the list of subjects has happened before, and it runs without trouble, resting on provisions of agency and attribution, not on the forest suddenly acquiring reason.

Cecil: By what nature did that forest obtain the status of subject? If not by reason, then by what?

〔Note〕Statute: Article 57 of the Civil Code (a legal person as an organization independently enjoying rights and bearing obligations according to law); Article 58 of the Civil Code (a legal person must have a name, organizational structure, domicile, and property or funds). Classics: Cicero, On Duties, Book I (persona as universal reason plus a particular role); the Tang Code with Commentary, "Names and General Principles" ("bondservants and the base, the law treats them as livestock"). Reality: section 11 of New Zealand's Te Urewera Act 2014 granted a natural object legal entity status, with a Board acting in its name.

### Round 4

Cecil: You cite that forest of 2014 and have walked half the way for me. You say the boundary between person and thing is only a movable line; I mean to walk it to the end and set one comparison across legal orders.

Scheien: Go ahead.

Cecil: Section 12 of the Te Awa Tupua (Whanganui River Claims Settlement) Act 2017 made the Whanganui River a legal person, enjoying the full rights and duties of a legal person, and at the same time set up Te Pou Tupua as its "human face" to act for it. The law grants the status because it wants someone able to claim for it and guard it.

Scheien: Does that not show that agency is enough?

Cecil: But does this river have reason, can it regret, can it answer for anything? Section 90a of the German Civil Code says animals are not things and the provisions on things apply mutatis mutandis; the legislator deliberately stopped there: what changed is treatment, not nature.

Scheien: I accept the German provision. By what does the boundary light up?

Cecil: Article 1(1) of the German Basic Law makes human dignity inviolable; Article 79(3) forbids even constitutional amendment to touch it. Set the two side by side and the boundary lights: a fiction can lengthen the list, it cannot rewrite a thing into a person. Aquinas, Summa Theologiae I q.29 a.1, says a person is an individual substance of a rational nature; the Xunzi, "On the Regulations of a King," draws the line at yi, and holds that only humans have yi, and are therefore the noblest under heaven.

Scheien: A role may be assigned; reason and yi may not. That I take. But behind a legal person stand natural persons who can convene, sign and answer; what Article 58 demands as "organizational structure" names exactly those people.

Cecil: You read a movable technical line as an effaceable line of nature.

Scheien: Then attribution. Are the rights and duties hung in the name of the river, or in the name of the person acting for it?

Cecil: The law answered itself: in the side where a human acts. Strip out Te Pou Tupua, that "human face," and can the river still appear in court, explain itself, be punished?

Scheien: It cannot. That seat I yield.

Cecil: Since it cannot, the "subject" you keep speaking of, who is it that is being subject there?

〔Note〕Statute: Section 90a of the German Civil Code (animals are not things; the provisions on things apply mutatis mutandis); Articles 1(1) and 79(3) of the German Basic Law (human dignity inviolable, not open even to constitutional amendment). Classics: Aquinas, Summa Theologiae I q.29 a.1 (a person is an individual substance of a rational nature); the Xunzi, "On the Regulations of a King" (the line drawn at yi). Reality: section 12 of New Zealand's Te Awa Tupua (Whanganui River Claims Settlement) Act 2017 granted the Whanganui River legal personality and set up Te Pou Tupua to act for it.

### Round 5

Scheien: You set up "a fiction can lengthen the list, it cannot rewrite a thing into a person" as a boundary stone, then asked how the river appears in court once the acting human is removed. I mean to dig from beneath that stone. The word "acting" is itself the hardest testimony.

Cecil: How so?

Scheien: Chapter 16 of Hobbes's Leviathan says it plainly: persona was a mask; the words and deeds of a fictitious person are borne by the representative; authorization and representation are the mechanism of personality. Personality never requires that the represented speak for itself, only that there be a set of rules attributing words and deeds to a name.

Cecil: Authorization requires someone able to authorize.

Scheien: And the same after authorization. A company cannot speak, yet it signs contracts, sues, is fined, and runs all the way through. The Company Law, revised in 2023, gives a company independent property in Article 3, and in Articles 10 and 11 lets the legal representative act in the company's name, with consequences borne by the company.

Cecil: Behind the company stand natural persons. I agree. That does not equal: the subject must be a natural person.

Scheien: Right, it only shows the chain of attribution needs a findable endpoint. The endpoint lands on the representative, the property and the registration, not on rational nature. Maine, in Ancient Law, presses this into one sentence: a fiction is the technique by which law adapts to society.

Cecil: Then speak with today's legislative fact.

Scheien: On 4 February 2016, the US NHTSA wrote to Google, interpreting an automated driving system as the "driver" for the federal motor vehicle safety standards. That is one clean interpretive fiction: the system did not become a human being; the law pointed the position "driver" at it, and the duty found a place to land.

Cecil: The position was given. The nature was not.

Scheien: Now the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, (EU) 2024/1689, generally applicable from 2 August 2026. The whole Act takes providers, deployers and importers as the bearers of duties; it grants AI no subject status whatsoever, and the entire Act runs perfectly well. The Commission's 2025 Digital Omnibus draft proposes postponing some high-risk obligations; I treat it as a draft only.

Cecil: So your answer is: the personality certificate need not be issued; write the point of attribution, the duty-bearer, the representative clearly, and that suffices.

Scheien: Registration, name, domicile, representative, liable property, winding-up: every one of them is a provision that can be written. My turn to ask: that letter calls the system "driver"; if by your standard that already rewrites a thing into a person, why has the whole body of motor vehicle safety standards not collapsed? "Assigning a position" and "changing a layer of nature": where do you draw that boundary?

〔Note〕Statute: Article 3 of the Company Law (2023 revision) (a company is a legal person with independent property); Articles 10 and 11 of the Company Law (the legal representative and the attribution of consequences); Article 113 of the EU Artificial Intelligence Act (EU) 2024/1689 (general application from 2 August 2026). Classics: Hobbes, Leviathan, ch. 16 (persona as a mask; authorization and representation); Maine, Ancient Law (a fiction as the technique by which law adapts to society). Reality: the NHTSA letter to Google of 4 February 2016, interpreting an automated driving system as the "driver."

### Round 6

Cecil: You take that NHTSA letter and ask why the standards have not collapsed, and you set "behind it stand natural persons" into the sentence "the chain of attribution needs an endpoint." I take up that word "endpoint"; the disagreement is exactly here.

Scheien: Then let us prise it apart with the burden of proof.

Cecil: The endpoint of the chain of attribution is not a coordinate; it is a person who can adduce evidence, can explain, can be punished. Article 48 of the E-Commerce Law says it most plainly: the acts of an automated information system in concluding or performing a contract take effect on the party using the system, and that party is presumed to have the corresponding capacity. The law does not hang the effect on the system.

Scheien: It hangs on the person.

Cecil: Article 61 of the Civil Code is the same: civil activities conducted by the legal representative in the name of the legal person have their consequences borne by the legal person. The bearer is still that organization whose accounts can be audited, which can be questioned, whose property can be executed.

Scheien: You cite Chapter 16 of Hobbes and omit the second half: the words and deeds of a fictitious person are borne by the representative, and authorization presupposes an authorizer able to authorize.

Cecil: A thing that cannot understand that it has been authorized, cannot explain why it acted so, cannot hold up an authorization relation. To hang liability in its name is to hand the burden of proof to a black box incapable of proving anything.

Scheien: And the one who really ought to explain walks free.

Cecil: The "banality of evil" that Arendt recorded in Eichmann in Jerusalem is exactly what things look like after "not judging" has pushed responsibility away layer by layer.

Scheien: You put it heavily.

Cecil: Heavy has heavy warrant. On 14 February 2024, the Civil Resolution Tribunal of British Columbia, in Moffatt v. Air Canada (2024 BCCRT 149), rejected the defence that "the chatbot is an independent legal entity" and ordered the airline to perform and to pay damages.

Cecil: It asked two things: who is using this system, and who ought to have made matters clear to the passenger. Your turn: if AI is registered as a point of attribution, then once it causes harm, from whom does the court obtain an explanation of "why it decided thus"? ### Round 7

Scheien: You close on "the one accused must be able to understand the accusation," then ask what it means to seat a subject that cannot recall and cannot answer in the defendant's chair. Let me give you another face of it: move "recall and answer" off the person and look only at how a rule recognizes a subject.

Cecil: Move it off. What is left?

Scheien: Locke, Essay Concerning Human Understanding II.xxvii §26, says it with great restraint: person is a forensic term, and personal identity lies in consciousness, not in substance.

Cecil: The consciousness he means is the kind that can claim its own past.

Scheien: He did not say identity lies in flesh and blood, nor in a soul-substance; he said consciousness. And consciousness is precisely what a legal order can use as a marker. Searle, in The Construction of Social Reality, sets out the constitutive rule "X counts as Y": a stack of paper counts as money, an entry of registration counts as a subject, resting on collective intentionality and rules, not on the fibres of the paper, nor on the silicon of the machine.

Cecil: Then run an experiment for me.

Scheien: Suppose a system backs up its parameters, conversation history and account state in full each day. One day the host burns out, and operations restores the same state from backup onto a new machine. Outwardly it keeps the same name, the same account, the same liable property, the same registration number.

Cecil: That point of attribution after restoration: two personalities, or one personality continued?

Scheien: By substance, two. The legal order recognizes only the registration number and property that can be continued, and treats it as one. The fact burned with the hardware; the technique lets the point of attribution live on.

Cecil: You have been driven into the corner, and you still have not said on what ground it is one.

Scheien: Article 13 of the Civil Code pins the civil capacity of natural persons to beginning at birth and ending at death; Article 14 says capacity is equal for all. These two are legislation writing the beginning and the end as rules, not depicting natural fact: birth and death are events, "begins at" and "ends at" are provisions.

Cecil: Provisions can write a beginning and an end; they cannot write "I am I."

Scheien: Reality is already forcing us to answer. Digital deceased and "AI resurrection" services are pushing likeness, voice, reputation and the personality interests of the dead onto the table, and the root of the dispute is whether the same set of data counts as the same person. Back to this backup case: if personality is a fact, is the restored system a new personality or the old one? Say the old: where is your "fact" deposited? Say the new: who answers for the old one's unfinished contracts and unsettled property?

〔Note〕Statute: Article 13 of the Civil Code (civil capacity of natural persons begins at birth and ends at death); Article 14 of the Civil Code (capacity is equal for all). Classics: Locke, Essay Concerning Human Understanding II.xxvii §26 (person as a forensic term; identity in consciousness); Searle, The Construction of Social Reality (the constitutive rule "X counts as Y"). ### Round 8

Cecil: That backup experiment is neat, and so is its conclusion: the fact burns with the hardware, the technique lets the point of attribution live on. You then borrow Locke to say identity lies in consciousness. That sentence I accept, but you have swapped three words: consciousness, data, identity.

Scheien: Take it apart.

Cecil: The consciousness Locke means is the kind that can claim its own past acts as "mine," can regret, can be questioned: self-concern of that sort. He is defending the same person able to answer, not the same set of copyable data. Scheien: I have read that line and broadly accept it.

Cecil: "The law treats it as one" is the convenience of an attributive technique; it does not equal "it is one." Scheien: You have converted continuity of registration into identity of personality, and that step is right. But I want to see your substitute.

### Round 9

Scheien: You left that question: shutting down the backup system, extinguishing a subject or deleting a piece of property. I choose the latter, and not to evade. Criminal law has already made the same kind of choice for us.

Cecil: Make it plain by analogy with the cases.

Cecil: And corporate crime?

Scheien: Articles 30 and 31 are better still. Corporate crime requires express statutory provision and adopts dual punishment: the unit is punished, and so are the directly responsible managers and other directly responsible personnel. Legislation opens a subject-shaped hole for this organization that cannot speak, and punishment always lands back on natural persons capable of intent and negligence. That is a ready-made specimen of limited, registrable, winding-up-able status.

Cecil: You read Kant's Rechtslehre 6:223 as a criterion: a person is a subject whose acts are imputable. Read as a criterion, how does it run?

### Round 10
